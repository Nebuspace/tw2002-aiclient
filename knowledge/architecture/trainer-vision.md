The client is not just a telnet front-end for an LLM to type into — it is a **trainer**: a
system that watches an AI pilot play, learns the profitable patterns behind what it sees, and
progressively takes over the keystrokes that no longer need a full reasoning pass. The end
state is a client that can earn without the AI actively driving it, having graduated from
demonstration to autonomous flight.

# The North Star

Two facets make up "the trainer," and neither collapses into the other:

- **The autonomous learner.** The AI demonstrates play; the client records what it did, mines
  the recordings for recurring profitable patterns, and replays those patterns deterministically
  — with zero LLM reasoning per cycle once a pattern is learned and trusted.
- **The strategy coach.** The trainer is equally a knowledgeable coach for the human operator: it
  encodes multiple TW2002 strategies (trade loops, port economics, exploration, combat/toll
  math, colonization) and surfaces contextual advice from the live game state — what applies
  right now, and why — rather than silently acting on the operator's behalf.

Both facets **are** the trainer. A trainer that only grinds loops without ever coaching the
operator, or only coaches without ever learning to fly itself, is half the vision.

# The Learning Pipeline

Demonstration becomes autonomy through one converged pipeline:

1. **Demonstrate.** The AI pilot plays live, driving the game through the client's one-round-trip
   verbs.
2. **Ledger.** Every action is recorded — the prompt it answered, the keystroke sent, the
   before/after game state, and the resulting reward (credits/turns/cargo delta).
3. **Record / replay a skill.** A demonstrated sequence can be captured as a named, parameterizable
   loop and replayed deterministically, validating each step's actual outcome against what was
   expected and halting on divergence rather than guessing.
4. **Mine.** The ledger is mined for recurring input sequences ranked by profitability (credits
   per turn, credits per action), surfacing candidate loops the AI never explicitly named as a
   "skill" but which repeat, profitably, in its own play.
5. **Play autonomously.** A learned loop runs unsupervised for a bounded number of cycles,
   streaming its own decision trace and halting on anything unexpected rather than pressing on
   through a surprise.
6. **Explore for more.** Exploration is a first-class appetite, not a fallback — the trainer
   spends a budgeted share of its attention seeking new profitable patterns rather than only
   replaying what it already knows, with the exploration rate rising as known loops deplete.

Each stage produces the fixtures the next stage needs — the pipeline cannot be reordered without
starving a later stage of the data it depends on.

# The Graduation Gauge

The trainer's progress toward "flying itself" is measured, not asserted: an **autonomy ratio**
tracks what share of keystrokes are generated by the deterministic trainer engine versus by the
AI's own reasoning. As learned loops replace AI-driven decisions, the ratio rises — the crossing
of a majority share is the concrete, measurable definition of "the trainer flies itself." See
[the autonomy loop](/architecture/autonomy-loop.md) for the metric's exact schema and gauge.

# Two Facets, One System

The autonomous-learner facet and the coaching facet share the same substrate — the persisted
[world model](/architecture/world-model.md) that both the deterministic engine and the coaching
advisor read from, and the same [session engine](/architecture/session-engine.md) that carries
every keystroke, whichever facet issued it. A coaching suggestion and an autonomously-replayed
loop are two outputs of one underlying knowledge base, not two separate systems bolted together.

# Alignment Ethos

The trainer's autonomous behavior is bounded by an explicit ethos, not left to whatever is
locally profitable: it never grieves another player and never initiates unprovoked conflict.
Where the operator's game world allows player conflict, the trainer's posture stays
protective-by-default — any player-facing combat, even in defense of another player, requires
the operator's own in-the-moment judgment rather than an autonomous trigger, because reliably
telling an aggressor from a victim in real time is a harder problem than the trainer currently
solves. The trainer's autonomy is real, but it is scoped to the economic and exploration game,
not to unsupervised conflict decisions.

# Examples

A graduation arc, illustrative:

```
Session 1: AI demonstrates a buy-Equipment/sell-Equipment pair between two adjacent sectors.
           actor=ai for every keystroke. Autonomy ratio: 0%.

Session 4: The pair has been recorded as a named loop and mined as a profitable pattern.
           Replaying it end-to-end runs as actor=trainer. Autonomy ratio: ~40%.

Session 9: Three loops are learned; the AI's role shrinks to scouting fresh source ports
           and supervising replay. Autonomy ratio crosses 50% — the trainer flies the
           majority of keystrokes for the first time.
```
